# Misrepresentation threats: protecting human understanding

**A record can be accurate while the impression it creates is wrong.** This companion to the [TUN threat model](THREAT-MODEL.md) adopts the contributed draft's focus on human understanding, outcome truth, recovery correctness and control efficacy.

**Reviewed baseline:** [64e7ce6](https://github.com/kochrisdev/TUN-Systemic-Design/tree/64e7ce6eb864deac1f0513a5d040a318116c959a), September 30, 2026. The original STRIDE register and its `TM-01`–`TM-18` identifiers remain unchanged. The seven `TM-M-*` identifiers retain the meanings from the contributed draft. See [reconciliation decisions](ATTACHMENT-RECONCILIATION.md) for changes to stale implementation and evidence statements.

## Method and responsibility

**M means misrepresentation**, a TUN-specific human-understanding lens, not a seventh standard STRIDE category. It can overlap with spoofing, tampering, denial of service or other STRIDE scenarios; a reviewer should assess both the misleading impression and its technical cause. [Microsoft's STRIDE definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/security/develop/threat-modeling-tool-threats) retain the six conventional categories.

Presentation safeguards reduce a misleading impression. They do not authenticate a host assertion, prove that a user understood it, or authorize execution. Host obligations below apply the existing specification to these scenarios; they do not introduce new specification MUSTs. Evidence pointers name existing assertions and scoped pilot evidence, not a declaration that every adopting service passes.

## Adoption guidance carried forward

**Render failure must not imply stopped work.** Validate hostile inputs before typed helpers and rendering. Isolate failure-prone TUN regions behind an application-owned error boundary and provide a separate authorized status/intervention surface that does not depend on the failed record or component subtree. A fallback should say that the view is unavailable, not that execution stopped. Test malformed payloads while work is in flight. The library does not currently supply this complete fallback; runtime-schema work and integration validation remain separate.

**Keep validation layers distinct.** Structural schemas, semantic checks, freshness/expiry policy, identity/permission enforcement and provider verification answer different questions. Adding schemas does not automatically make `proposalBlockReason` or recovery policy redundant, nor does it resolve an unreported side effect.

**Review understanding, not just markup.** Ask a user what they believe occurred after acknowledgement, timeout, partial completion and compensation. Pair the technical assertion with their answer and the actual service record. The [W3C use-of-color guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html) supports an additional visible means of conveying color-coded information; it does not establish that a particular label is understood.

The draft's proposals for stricter expiry/consequence linting and mandatory typed confirmation need separate specification/API review. They are not silently added as requirements here. Keep live repository-control status in [Security maintenance](SECURITY-MAINTENANCE.md), not copied into another undated checklist.

## Maintenance

Keep these M identifiers stable and cross-reference related STRIDE threats. Update presentation statements and evidence pointers when source changes, and record the assessed revision. The component/threat checker validates table definitions and relation references, not the semantic completeness of this analysis or arbitrary test-title prose. Review those claims against source and named reports. The [attachment review](ATTACHMENT-RECONCILIATION.md) records the full legacy threat crosswalk.
